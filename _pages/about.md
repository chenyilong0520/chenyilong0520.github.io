Yilong Chen is an Ph.D student in Robotics & Intelligent Construction Automation Lab (RICAL) at Georgia Tech. Previously, he received a B. Eng. degree from Zhejiang University and three M. S. degrees from the University of Tokyo, UT Austin, and Georgia Tech. Before enrolled in the Ph.D program, he worked as a BIM software engineer at Applied Technology (応用技術株式会社) in Japan. 

His work sits where robot 3D perception, spatial memory and navigation meet. He builds real-time, LiDAR-centric systems that let robots work safely among people in changing environments. These include LiDAR–camera fusion for tracking workers, a temporal scene graph that detects dynamic changes on an onboard CPU, and graph neural networks that learn human-aware path corrections from unlabeled demonstrations and transfer from simulation to a quadruped robot. He ships working ROS software and tests it with human participants. He has authored/co-authored 3 SCI journal paper, and 6 conference papers.
